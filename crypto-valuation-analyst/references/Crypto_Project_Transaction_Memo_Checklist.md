## Purpose

Repeatable work procedure to evaluate narratives, tokenomics as the capital structure, on-chain fundamentals, security, and competitive positioning. Systematically define your strategy like a banker underwriting a business.

**Goal:** Adapt traditional company analysis (narrative, SWOT with mitigants, benchmarking, and decision criteria) to crypto realities.

---

## 1. Transaction Proposal Summary

Defines success and failure of the future investment and anchors research.

- **Core Goal:**
  - Speculative asymmetric upside
  - Long-term hold
  - Yield and governance participation
  - Narrative rotation trade
- **Time Horizon:** days/weeks - trading, 3-12 months - swing trading, 1+ years - conviction
- **Success Metrics:**
  - Achieve a specific return by the end of the time horizon
  - Protocol Total Value Locked (TVL) or user growth by a specific amount
  - Token utility adoption: fees captured and staking income
  - Specific catalyst or news event
- **Failure Metrics:**
  - Stop loss
  - Regulatory change
  - Token inflation
  - Team exits
- **Position Sizing:**
  - Max % of portfolio size
  - Max loss tolerance
  - Scaling plan: when to buy more or sell portions
- **Entry, Accumulation, Profit Taking, and Exit Zones:** price levels, market cap targets, or condition based (such as post-unlock stabilization)
- **Total Dollar Value Risked**
- **Maximum Possible Gain and Loss**
- **Edge:** Why does this opportunity exist? (Information asymmetry, narrative mispricing, or execution edge vs market)

## 2. Proposal Theme

- Broad themes like AI, DeFi, and Real World Assets (RWA) are only starting points.
- Narrow the theme to a specific narrative with short-term catalysts. (Example: Decentralized AI agents on Solana with verifiable compute, not AI crypto)
- **Type**
  - Store of Value, Smart Contract, Stablecoin, Utility, Governance, Application Layer, etc
  - Consensus Mechanism: Proof of Work (PoW), Stake (PoS), History (PoH), Authority (PoA), Burn (PoB), etc
- **Where are we in the narrative cycle?**
  - Early → Momentum → Peak → Exit → Dormant (Possibly Undervalued)
  - Notice how this is similar to the classic cycle bubbles follow. You can still profit during the run-up of momentum (a feedback loop known as reflexivity), but your risk increases exponentially as you try to sell to the next greater fool.
- **Macro Cycle Fit:** Does this fit the current Bull/Bear market? Is sector rotation favoring this narrative now? Are there imminent regulatory threats?
- Would this project stand out in a bear market or a crowded narrative? If not, we may require better historical performance or tokenomics metrics relative to comps.

## 3. Project Background

These are narrative-based factors that may help or harm your proposal. I attempt to find novel ways to measure them quantitatively, but ultimately your judgement and interpretation is necessary to grade them.

### 3.1 Team, Founders, and Execution

- Has the team been doxxed and do they have verifiable identities or track records?
  - Quantified through the team’s LinkedIn and GitHub.
  - Does the team have successful past exits from other projects? Did the project stay afloat following their departure?
- Conduct this analysis for other related parties (advisors, VC partners, managers of related corporate entities, associates of the team, major holders, community contributors, founders) if they are identifiable.
  - Are the team members and related parties’ interests aligned with the community? This is demonstrated by a token allocation and gradual vesting schedule.
- Is the project responding to the community?

### 3.2 Community, Distribution, and Governance

- Discussion quality on Telegram, Discord, or X: organic vs bot activity
  - Can be measured with AIGC detection and sentiment analysis tools.
- Active governance: Real proposals and voting turnout
- Developer Ecosystem: GitHub activity (number of forks, likes, contributors, and PRs)
- Distribution: Is there a community driven organic growth loop or purely incentive driven? (Chasing returns or unlocks)

### 3.3 Product, Tech, and Security

- Product-Market Fit: Is there verifiable demand for the project?
  - Can be measured via the Tokenholder Retention Rate: What percentage of wallets from a year ago transacted again in the following year?
- Smart Contract Audits: Determine the number of companies involved and the percentage of findings requiring remediation.
- Code Quality: Verify open source and active development on GitHub.
- Upgradability and Control: Smart contract timelocks and multi-signature provide defense against malicious actors and unfavorable governance actions. What is the time until control is decentralized, if any?
- Dependencies and Systemic Risks:
  - Reliance on off-chain sources (oracles) add vulnerability and centralization risk.
  - Bridge Risk: What facilitates moving the token across different blockchains? Has there been an audit completed?
  - Merger Risk: Is there a rumored merger and what will its impact be on the performance of the token?
- Regulatory Risk and Legal Exposure: Determine past enforcement actions and civil suits for the project and related parties. Is there a clear legal structure?

### 3.4 Competitive Landscape, Moat, and SWOT with Mitigants

Every negative factor must be offset with a credible positive mitigant. If you find yourself reaching for a tangential positive, this is a good sign that you have an unmitigated red flag.

- **SWOT:**
  - Internal Strengths and Weaknesses
  - External Opportunities and Threats
  - Credible positive mitigants for each weakness and threat.
- **Porter’s Five Forces:**
  - Threat of New Entrants (forks)
  - Threat of Substitutes (other chains)
  - User Power (governance)
  - Supplier Power (validators and oracles)
  - Rivalry Intensity
- **Sustainable Moat:** What prevents new forks and chains from threatening the market position of the target project? (Examples: Tech, Intellectual Property, Network Effects, Branding. Partnerships)

## 4. On-Chain Analysis: The Crypto Financial Statements

On-chain data and tokenomics metrics act as the financial statements for most crypto projects. Focus on trends, quality, and sustainability instead of absolute numbers.

### 4.1 Market and Fundamental Metrics

- Fully Diluted Valuation (FDV) = Token Price X Total Supply
- Market Cap / FDV Ratio quantifies how much more supply is still coming into the market.
  - Above 60%: Most tokens are already circulating. Low expected dilution.
  - Range of 60% to 30%: Moderate dilution expected.
  - Range of 30% to 10%: High dilution risk.
  - Less than 10%: Extreme dilution risk. Red flag for new launches.
- 1D, 1W, 3M, 1Y performance and key drivers
- Liquidity Depth: CEX + DEX depth as a % of market cap. Depth being <0.5% of market cap implies high slippage risk and difficult large entries and exits.
- Trading Volume Quality: Organic vs wash trading / manipulation indicators?
- Tokenholder Metrics:
  - Concentration: Top 10, Top 100, and Total Wallets - Can be measured with Arkham, Nansen, or on-chain explorers.
  - Total Holders Trend: Is concentration improving or worsening?
  - New Holder Growth and Churn: Is the amount of tokenholders dependent on growth exceeding a high churn rate?
- Usage: Daily and Monthly Active User Trend and Tokenholder Retention Rate
- Velocity and Staking:
  - Velocity = Total Transaction Volume ($) / Market Cap
  - Staking %: What percentage of tokenholders are staked?
  - Low velocity and high staking % indicates holders are long-term.
- TVL Trend and Quality: Is capital sticky or opportunistic?
  - Can determine stickiness over time with an adaptation of the Price Elasticity of Demand where (%Δ TVL / %Δ Price) below 1 indicates that capital is sticky.
- Revenue Generated: Protocol revenue for DeFi and fees + mining rewards for PoW. Who captures revenue (miners, treasury, stakers, burns)?
  - Adapting Price / Sales or Revenue Multiple: Price / Revenue

### 4.2 Tokenomics and Capital Structure

Token allocation + vesting + float is the closest analog to the traditional cap table. Use this to assess alignment, dilution risk, and launch signal quality.

**Double Edged Sword:** Founders and related parties having high allocation with gradual vesting can be a positive sign for principal-agent alignment. When it’s excessive or vested as a cliff, this can be a sign of trouble in the project’s future. A low allocation can also be a signal of noncommitment, but is safer from a dilution standpoint. Weigh these tradeoffs. The most balanced option is to look for an allocation and vesting schedule similar to successful comps or a market average.

**Example: Typical Token Cap Table at Launch**

| Category | Typical % | Purpose | Vesting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Community / Ecosystem | 20-50%+ | Airdrops, liquidity, user incentives | Immediate or gradual |
| Treasury / Foundation | 20-40% | Development, grants, operations, liquidity | DAO / multi-sig controlled |
| Team / Founders | 10-20% | Long-term alignment and retention | 1-4 years, cliffs are common |
| Investors | 15-30% | Early capital from VCs, likely SAFEs | 2-4 years, cliffs are common |

Example: Uniswap (UNI) at launch allocated ~45-60% to community/governance, ~22% to the team (4 year vesting), and ~18% to investors (4 year vesting).

What claims on the legal entity of the project or the tokens themselves are senior to the community tokenholders? What percentage is in Simple Agreements for Future Equity (SAFEs)? How much in the future can be committed to senior claims?

- Verify allocation structure from the whitepaper, tokenomics docs, and on-chain data.
- Map the complete vesting/unlock schedule, cliffs, and cadence. Use previous data from the related parties to determine if they have a history of immediate exits.
- Calculate initial circulating supply at the Token Generation Event (TGE) as % of total supply. This is an alternative measure of future dilution where a higher float at the TGE is a positive signal.

### 4.3 Forecasting, Valuation, Benchmarking, and Stress Testing

- **Scenario Valuation:**
  - Forecast Base, Bull, and Bear cases within your time horizon.
  - Explicit Assumptions: adoption, revenue, and unlock rate
  - Change assumption variables to test how sensitive the valuation is to the assumptions.
  - Probability weights based on judgements of the likelihood of each case.
- **Price Floors or Support Levels:**
  - Consider the fact that an altcoin has a natural tendency to go to zero in the absence of a price floor. The floor could be -99.9%.
  - What is the value of an underlying collateral?
  - Intrinsic Price Floor: cost to mine for a PoW token or a DCF style capitalization of revenue for PoS
  - Discount Rate: Should mirror the discount rates for a start-up (20% - 70% depending on the growth stage).
  - The Bear case forecast should take the price floor into account.
- **Stress Test Analysis:**
  - Model how dilution from team and investor unlocks will impact price.
  - Market Cap / Float = Current Price
  - Market Cap / Projected Float After Unlocks = Theoretical Diluted Price (all other factors held equal)
- **Comparables Analysis:**
  - Similar in growth stage, sector, and financial metrics (MC / FDV, Price / Revenue).
  - Compare premium or discount relative to the project’s narrative.
  - Compare the strength of the community and cap table.
  - What would the target project be worth if it were valued at the same multiple as the comp set average or median?
- **Test Correlation:**
  - With blue-chip cryptos (ETH or BTC)
  - With tech sector stocks (NASDAQ 100)
  - With commodities (Gold, Oil, Electricity)
  - Should we forecast price as a percentage of another asset? Consider the idea that Bitcoin is “digital gold”.
  - Can the correlations be used to hedge your position? (Example: Spend 10% of the trade on a Bitcoin downside prediction contract or BITO put options after observing high correlation)
- **Review:** Find the high, low, average, and median valuations from the scenario valuations, price floors, support levels, stress-tested valuation, and comparables market-based valuation.

## 5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Red Flags and Risk Mitigation

- High team and investor allocations (>40%) with short vesting (<2 years) or large short term cliffs. Anonymous team. Large whale concentrations in addition to this.
- Treasury is an unknown small group with unusual spending patterns.
- Low float (<20%) at TGE if a new token.
- Security incidents with poor incident response. No smart contract auditing or a limited number of auditors. Unremediated audit findings. Hashrate declines beyond historical trends.
- Systemic Dependencies
- Narrative hype causing an overextension of price beyond what the product’s utility or fundamental metrics can support prior to entry.
- Manipulation Risks: low liquidity, high concentration
- Legal Issues
- Risk Classification
  - Unrelated to projected growth prospects
  - Related but mitigated with fundamental strengths, expected team actions, or specific actions, such as hedging.
  - Unmitigated risk

### 5.2 Scoring and Decision

- **Hurdle Rate:** Does the projected growth and potential downside outperform other uses of funds?
  - Blue-Chip Crypto: BTC, ETC
  - Broad Equities: S&P 500, FTSE 100, etc
  - Cash: US Treasury Bills
  - Personal Hurdle Rate: What is the minimum possible percentage gain you need to compensate for the risk?
- **Inflection Points:**
  - Entry price range
  - Accumulation price range to buy more
  - Exits: Stop-loss and profit-taking ranges
- **Position Size:** Should reflect a percentage of the total portfolio, risk tolerance, and conviction level.
- **Monitoring Plan:** How frequently should price be checked? Set calendar alerts for unlocks, governance news sources, and key price levels.
- **Commentary on Decision Rationale:** Why this project, why now, what needs to go right, and what would invalidate the decision.
